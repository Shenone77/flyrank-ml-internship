Catching material search declines with ten reviews per client

A time-based test of which pages to send to an editor first, using two months of observed outcomes from FlyRank’s search warehouse.

Abstract

When a content editor can review ten pages per client, which pages should enter the queue first? I used two pre-decision feature windows and their observed 30-day outcomes from FlyRank’s pseudonymized search warehouse. I trained a small logistic ranker on April outcomes and compared it with a fixed exposure rule on June outcomes. Across 32 clients, mean Precision@10 was 68.1% for logistic ranking and 32.2% for the exposure rule. The findings support a human-review pilot with a fixed client budget and a separate check for unusually large visibility losses.

The editorial decision

An editor has ten checks for each client and wants to identify pages at risk of losing meaningful search visibility. A page enters the candidate set only if it had at least 300 impressions in the prior 30 days. The output is a ranked list with a reason code and a practical first check. A false alarm takes a review slot, while a missed drop delays investigation.

Data and coverage

I used FlyRank’s v20260703 warehouse release. I aggregated the daily performance fact to one page per client per cutoff, joined the client dimension for tracking start, and joined the content dimension for creation date. A candidate needed tracking before or at the start of the 30-day feature window, 30 observed GSC days, a known creation date, and at least 300 impressions.

Methodology

Outcome. A material drop requires both at least 150 fewer impressions and at least 30% fewer impressions in the following 30 days. The preceding 30 days supply the comparison level. The thresholds define which observed losses matter for this editorial policy.

Comparator and model. The exposure rule ranks by prior impressions and applies a 25% boost when prior CTR is at most 0.5%. Logistic regression learns from log prior impressions, prior CTR, age at the cutoff, the number of prior days with impressions, and the share of prior impressions in the last 15 days. Scaling and model fitting used only the training set. Both methods score the same June rows.

Validation. I checked the page-day grain and required GSC availability to be true. Features end before the outcome window begins. Pseudonymous IDs serve joins and client-level grouping. Recent update fields and post-cutoff measurements stay outside the feature frame. Mean Precision@10 gives equal weight to each client with at least ten observable candidates. The pooled top 50 is a secondary view. Exact score ties use the same seed-based ordering.

More useful picks per client

Logistic ranking found 218 material drops in 320 review slots. The rule found 103. The mean difference was 35.9 percentage points, with a paired client-bootstrap interval of 28.4 to 43.8 points in this holdout. Logistic ranking improved 29 client queues and tied three. Random selection had an expected mean precision of 34.7% under the same client allocation.

Precision and lost impressions tell different stories. The pooled rule surfaced 22 confirmed drops representing 2.03 million lost impressions. The logistic queue surfaced 48 drops representing 0.63 million. The rule concentrated more of its effort on unusually high-exposure pages. These are measured losses associated with the selected pages.

Lower late-window impression share had the largest standardized coefficient in magnitude. Prior active days had a zero fitted coefficient. The date-based result applies to the observed June window. The holdout may contain clients seen in training.

Limitations and honest framing

In June, 10,484 eligible pages, about 16.1%, lacked a complete follow-up. If missingness is associated with removal or lost tracking, precision among observable pages may differ from the live queue. The outcome rate rose from 37.2% in training to 51.6% among observable June pages, and the client mix also changed. A single time holdout provides directional evidence. The paired interval reflects variation across clients in that window.

A drop can reflect demand, ranking, indexing, a migration, or measurement. The study measures which pages were surfaced under a fixed definition. A refresh experiment would be needed to measure recovery.

Ranked review actions

  1. Review fading visibility first. For high-ranked pages whose final 15 days account for under 35% of prior impressions, check indexing, seasonality, and demand before choosing an intervention.
  2. Check low-CTR pages. Inspect query mix, search-result presentation, and snippet fit. Confirm that the impressions came from a market and intent worth serving.
  3. Review remaining high-ranked pages. Keep the queue at ten per client. Flag an unusually high-exposure page for manual comparison within that budget, especially when the two rankings disagree.
  4. Monitor the rest. Log findings and review time. Recheck Precision@10 versus the rule after each labeled window and revisit the model if it trails the rule for two windows.
